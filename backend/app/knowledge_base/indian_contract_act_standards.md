# Indian Contract Act 1872 Compliance Benchmarks

## Section 10: Essentials of a Valid Contract
- **Requirement**: A valid contract requires free consent (Section 13), competent parties (Section 11), lawful consideration (Section 23), and a lawful object (Section 23).
- **Void Contracts**: Covenants that are unlawful, immoral, or oppose public policy are void.

## Section 27: Agreements in Restraint of Trade
- **Requirement**: Any agreement that restrains anyone from exercising a lawful profession, trade, or business of any kind is, to that extent, void.
- **Non-Compete Covenants**: Post-termination non-compete clauses are generally void and unenforceable under Indian law. Only in-term non-compete covenants (during employment/engagement) are valid.

## Sections 124 & 125: Contracts of Indemnity
- **Requirement**: Indemnity under Indian law is a promise to save the indemnified party from loss caused to them by the conduct of the promisor or any third party.
- **Scope**: Direct losses resulting from breach are covered. Indirect/consequential losses are generally excluded unless specifically agreed upon in the contract.
- **Rule of Fairness**: Mutual indemnity is standard. One-sided indemnity clauses where a weaker party indemnifies a stronger party for the stronger party's own negligence are highly discouraged and flagged as high-risk.

## Section 74: Liquidated Damages and Penalties
- **Requirement**: If a sum is named in the contract as the amount to be paid in case of breach, or if the contract contains any other stipulation by way of penalty, the party complaining of the breach is entitled to receive reasonable compensation not exceeding the amount so named.
- **Unreasonable Interest/Late Fees**: Interest rates on late payments exceeding 1.5% to 2% per month (18%-24% per year) are generally viewed as punitive. Courts will only award actual proven loss, not excessive penalty amounts.
